This is a strong idea: an AI friendship and learning companion that can communicate through voice, text, and sign language using a cute anime-style avatar.

1. Main capabilities
Your agent could support:

Normal conversation

User speaks or types.
AI understands the message.
Avatar replies with voice, text, facial expressions, and gestures.
Sign-language conversation

User signs in front of a camera.
AI recognizes the signs.
It responds using animated sign-language movements, text, or speech.
Sign-language learning

Teach individual signs, phrases, grammar, and fingerspelling.
Show a sign slowly.
Let the learner copy it.
Use the camera to provide feedback.
Include quizzes, repetition, and progress tracking.
Friend-like personality

Friendly anime character.
Remembers the learner’s name, level, goals, and previous lessons.
Encourages the user without pretending to be a real human.
Allows users to select personality, voice, clothing, and avatar style.
2. Important decision: choose one sign language first
Sign languages are not universal. For example:

ASL — American Sign Language
BSL — British Sign Language
ISL — Indian Sign Language
Auslan — Australian Sign Language
They have different vocabulary, grammar, and hand movements.

For the first version, choose one language, such as ASL or the sign language used by your target audience. Do not translate ordinary spoken-language grammar word-for-word into signs. Sign languages have their own grammar and structure.

You should work with:

Deaf educators
Native signers
Sign-language interpreters
Deaf community organizations
This is especially important for accurate teaching and respectful representation.

5. Sign-language output is the hardest part
There are two possible approaches.

Option A: Pre-recorded signs
Create a library of signs performed by a professional signer.

Advantages:

More accurate
Easier to validate
Better for an educational MVP
Disadvantages:

Limited vocabulary
Less flexible conversation
Option B: Animated sign-language avatar
Convert sign-language phrases into animation using a rigged 3D avatar.

Advantages:

More flexible
Can support larger vocabularies
Better for real-time interaction
Disadvantages:

Much harder
Requires sign-language animation data
Must be reviewed by native signers
A good product can combine both: use pre-recorded or professionally captured signs for important educational content and generated animation for less critical responses.

7. Example conversation behavior
User: “Hi, I want to learn sign language.”

Avatar:

“Hi! I’m Hana. I can help you practice. Would you like to learn greetings, numbers, or fingerspelling?”

The avatar then:

Shows the sign
Says and displays the meaning
Explains hand position
Plays it slowly
Asks the user to copy it
Checks the user’s movement
Gives supportive feedback
The feedback should be careful:

“Your hand shape looks close. Try moving your hand slightly higher.”

Avoid saying:

“Your sign is wrong.”

unless the system is highly reliable.

8. AI personality design
Create a personality specification such as:

Kind and patient
Friendly but not emotionally manipulative
Encouraging
Uses simple explanations for beginners
Never makes fun of mistakes
Admits uncertainty
Does not claim to be Deaf or a native signer
Clearly says when a sign needs verification
Encourages learning from qualified Deaf teachers
The avatar can feel like a friend, but it should not encourage unhealthy emotional dependence or pretend to replace human relationships and professional education.

11. Most important product principles
Start with one sign language.
Involve Deaf people from the beginning.
Use native signers to validate every lesson.
Treat facial expressions and body movement as part of the language.
Avoid claiming that basic gesture recognition equals full translation.
Use an original anime character rather than copying an existing character.
Make the avatar’s hands large and visually clear.
Provide captions and text for every spoken response.
Include privacy controls because camera and voice data are sensitive.
Make the system useful even when recognition fails.
A realistic first goal would be:

“A friendly anime tutor that teaches beginner ASL through voice, text, professionally validated sign demonstrations, and limited camera-based practice.”

That is achievable and can later develop into a full two-way sign-language companion.
